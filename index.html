How I Solve Problems

I understand the problem deeply before I draw a single pixel.

Most design failures start with solving the wrong problem. My process front-loads understanding — listening to users, mapping the system, and defining the real constraint — so the solution that follows is the right one, not just the obvious one.

01

Listen & Frame

User interviews, support tickets, analytics. I turn noise into a sharp problem statement everyone can rally around.

02

Map the System

Flows, journeys, information architecture. I find where the real friction lives — not where the complaints point.

03

Design & Test

Prototype early, test with real users, iterate on evidence. I'd rather be wrong in Figma than in production.

04

Ship & Measure

Partner with engineering to ship, then watch the metrics. A solution isn't done until the number moves.

About

A problem-solver first, a designer second.

For over nine years I've helped fintech, healthcare, and SaaS teams turn tangled, high-stakes problems into products people actually understand. I'm happiest in the messy middle — the interviews, the edge cases, the trade-offs — where good design is really decided.

I believe the best design is invisible: it removes friction so quietly that people simply get done what they came to do. My work lives at the intersection of research, systems thinking, and craft, and I measure success in outcomes — drop-off reduced, tasks completed, confidence restored.

When I'm not designing, I write about design process, mentor early-career designers, and take very long walks with a very opinionated dog.
